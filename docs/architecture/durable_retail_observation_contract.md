# Durable Retail Observation Contract

**Status:** Contract design only. This document does not select a database,
modify executable models, or create a persistence implementation.

## 1. Scope

This contract defines the database-independent semantics required to preserve
historical retail observations. It separates source identity, immutable
observations, commercial signals, capture context, evidence, and derived
interpretations.

It does not reopen Product or ProductVariant identity. It does not define a
database schema, indexes, price comparison, offer evaluation, or cart
optimization.

## 2. Existing Contracts Reused

The contract reuses these executable boundaries:

- `NormalizedObservation` is the immutable normalized ingestion record and is
  currently persisted by `FilesystemObservationRegistry` using
  `observation_id`.
- `RawArtifactReference` identifies the immutable captured artifact.
- `CaptureContext` carries country, currency, locale, location scope, session
  scope, and additional parameters at the `ScrapeJob` boundary.
- `PlatformListing` identifies a platform-native listing.
- `ListingObservation` is currently a Product Intelligence projection of a
  normalized observation, not an independently persisted registry record.
- `CanonicalListingAssociation` records a resolved observation-to-Product /
  ProductVariant mapping.
- `EvidenceReference`, `ObservationFieldReference`, and `EvidenceRegistry`
  remain the provenance authorities.
- `Money` and Cost Intelligence evaluator result types represent typed
  monetary/evaluated values, but do not currently define raw retail price
  persistence semantics.

## 3. Durable ListingObservation Definition

A durable retail listing observation is an immutable record of what a platform
listing exposed at one capture event. It identifies:

- platform;
- platform listing identity;
- capture timestamp;
- capture location and context;
- observed price signals;
- observed availability signal;
- observed offer text;
- parser and normalization versions;
- raw artifact and evidence references;
- canonical ProductVariant association when one was already governed and
  resolved.

The observation remains valid when canonical association is unresolved,
ambiguous, or conflicting. Resolution status must not cause the historical
commercial observation to be deleted or rewritten. An unresolved observation
must not be treated as a canonical ProductVariant observation for comparison.

In the current implementation, `NormalizedObservation` carries most of this
record. `ListingObservation` omits platform, observation identity, canonical
IDs, currency, and explicit location, so it is currently a downstream
projection rather than the complete durable record.

## 4. Observation Identity

`NormalizedObservation.observation_id` remains the upstream immutable identity
for the normalized observation and is generated by the existing
`NormalizedObservationIdentityBuilder`.

The durable retail-observation identity must distinguish separate semantic
capture events for the same platform listing while allowing exact replay of the
same event to be idempotent.

The following are source or event dimensions and must be considered by the
owner-defined observation identity policy:

- platform and platform listing/source record identity;
- capture context, including location and currency;
- capture timestamp or an explicitly governed capture-event identity;
- observed commercial signals;
- artifact identity where needed to distinguish captures.

Parser and normalization versions are provenance and replay inputs. Whether
they participate directly in durable listing-observation identity, beyond the
existing normalized `observation_id`, is **OPEN — requires product/architecture
decision**.

Identity must not use filesystem paths, worker identity, process identity, or
runtime timing metadata. Canonical Product and ProductVariant IDs are
association targets, not substitutes for source observation identity.

For an existing identity:

- identical payload is an idempotent registration;
- different payload is a conflict and must fail closed;
- stored observations are immutable;
- corrections are new governed observations or explicit correction records;
  overwrite behavior is not allowed.

The exact durable identity key is **OPEN** because the current
`ListingObservation` model has no identity field or identity builder and the
existing normalized identity currently excludes capture timestamp and capture
context.

## 5. Typed Price Semantics

The current normalized fields are deliberately textual:

- `observed_price_text` is the observed selling/display price text;
- `observed_mrp_text` is the observed reference-price text;
- `observed_offer_text` is offer text, not a calculated discount.

For a future durable typed representation:

- numeric amount must be represented without floating-point ambiguity;
- currency must be explicit and compatible with the capture context;
- selling price and reference/MRP price must remain distinct facts;
- raw text must be preserved when parsing is incomplete or fails;
- a missing or unparseable price remains unknown, not zero;
- no discount may be calculated solely from two displayed text values.

The repository's `Money` type uses integer minor units and a three-letter
currency code. Whether raw retail observations must adopt that representation,
what minor-unit/precision policy applies to every supported currency, and
whether observed prices include tax are **OPEN — require product/financial
decision**.

Tax inclusion, payable-price semantics, settlement price, and currency
conversion are not established by the current observation pipeline.

## 6. Availability Semantics

`availability_signal` is a platform/UI signal. It is not a warehouse
inventory assertion and must not be interpreted as guaranteed stock.

The durable observation may preserve a governed availability classification:

- in stock;
- out of stock;
- unavailable;
- unknown.

The mapping from platform strings to these classifications is
**OPEN — requires a governed platform mapping**. Until mapped, the original
signal remains evidence and the normalized state is unknown.

An availability change is a new observation event when captured at a distinct
capture event. Availability history is append-only. Location and delivery
context are relevant dimensions because availability can differ by location;
the exact identity treatment is part of the observation identity decision.

No inventory quantity, warehouse state, reservation state, or fulfillment
guarantee is implied.

## 7. Offer Semantics

Observed offer text and observed price are separate facts:

`observed price` does not prove `offer explains price`.

The current parser and normalizer preserve offer text only. Cost Intelligence
contains evaluator vocabulary for fixed discounts, percentage discounts,
cashback, wallet credit, fees, memberships, and unknown outcomes, but these
types represent evaluated inputs/results and do not establish a durable offer
record or eligibility rule.

A future durable offer record must preserve, where available:

- source/evidence identity;
- observed offer text;
- governed offer type;
- eligibility and membership/coupon requirements;
- validity interval or capture-time applicability;
- relationship to the observed price.

Offer identity, validity, eligibility, coupon semantics, membership semantics,
and whether an offer is attached to a listing observation or checkout context
are **OPEN**. Unknown offers must never be treated as no offer or as a zero
discount.

## 8. Capture Context

Capture context is supplied by `ScrapeJob.capture_context` and includes:

- country code;
- currency code;
- locale;
- location scope;
- session scope;
- canonical additional parameters.

The observation retains its `RawArtifactReference`, including capture time,
platform, capture type, and source reference. A durable observation must retain
or reference the applicable capture context without duplicating mutable context
values unnecessarily.

Currency and location are required for historical comparability. Session scope
is provenance/context and must not be interpreted as a customer identity.

The exact durable context identity, delivery-slot semantics, membership state,
and whether context changes create a new observation identity are **OPEN**.

## 9. Evidence and Provenance

Every durable commercial observation must remain explainable through:

- `observation_id`;
- `RawArtifactReference` and artifact digest;
- `EvidenceReference`;
- `ObservationFieldReference`;
- platform and platform listing identity;
- capture timestamp and context;
- parser version;
- normalization version;
- canonical association, when resolved.

The Evidence Registry remains the evidence authority. A durable observation
store must reference evidence and artifacts rather than copying raw bytes or
creating a second evidence system.

## 10. Historical and Append-Only Semantics

Retail observations are immutable historical facts as captured. Persistence
must be append-only at the observation level:

- replay of the same observation is idempotent;
- conflicting payload under the same observation identity fails closed;
- a later price, availability, offer, or context is a new observation;
- latest observation is a query/projection, never the source of truth;
- historical observations are not rewritten when catalog data or listing
  associations change.

The repository does not currently define correction records, supersession
records, or a formal temporal association model. Those are **OPEN** if Cartel
needs corrections to be distinguished from ordinary later observations.

## 11. Listing Association Semantics

The conceptual relationship is:

```text
PlatformListing
    <- platform + platform_listing_id
ListingObservation
    <- one immutable capture event
CanonicalListingAssociation
    -> canonical_product_id + canonical_variant_id
```

The existing association registry requires both canonical IDs, preserves the
observation ID, rejects conflicting mappings, and does not silently reassign a
listing. Candidate snapshots contain canonical Products and Variants, not
listing observations.

Therefore:

- one platform may have many listings for one canonical Variant;
- unresolved observations may remain durable but are not associated;
- a conflicting current listing mapping fails closed;
- historical observations must not be rewritten by later association changes;
- listing retirement behavior is **OPEN**, because `PlatformListing` has no
  lifecycle field and the current association contract does not define
  retirement persistence.

Whether a listing may change canonical Variant through a governed correction,
how historical association versions are represented, and whether a listing can
be reactivated are **OPEN**. This document does not change the existing
association contract.

## 12. Required Queries

The future durable boundary must support these semantic queries:

| Query | Required dimensions |
|---|---|
| Latest observation for a listing | platform, listing ID, capture ordering |
| Latest comparable price for a Variant | canonical Variant, location/context, currency, typed price eligibility |
| Price history for a Variant | canonical Variant association, capture time, price, context, evidence |
| Price history for a platform listing | platform, listing ID, capture time |
| Availability history | listing or Variant, location/context, capture time, governed availability |
| Active/relevant offers | listing/observation, offer identity, validity, eligibility, evidence |
| Cross-platform comparison | canonical Variant, platform, location/context, comparable currency and price semantics |
| Time-window observations | capture time, platform/listing or canonical Variant |
| Context-specific observations | location, currency, delivery context, membership context where governed |
| Evidence for a commercial observation | observation identity, field references, artifact/evidence references |

These are query requirements, not schema or index prescriptions.

## 13. Explicit OPEN Questions

1. Exact durable ListingObservation identity and its relationship to
   `NormalizedObservation.observation_id`.
2. Whether capture timestamp, location, currency, artifact identity, parser
   version, and normalization version participate directly in that identity.
3. Typed retail-price representation, currency precision, and tax inclusion.
4. Mapping platform availability signals to governed availability states.
5. Offer identity, validity, eligibility, and membership/coupon semantics.
6. Durable capture-context identity and delivery-context semantics.
7. Correction/supersession representation for observations and associations.
8. Listing retirement and reactivation semantics.
9. Whether review cases and assertion history are durable domain state.
10. Required retention and query performance targets for historical data.

## 14. Non-Goals

This contract does not:

- choose PostgreSQL, SQLite, or another storage technology;
- define tables, indexes, migrations, or ORM models;
- change Product or ProductVariant identity;
- interpret free-form offer or availability text beyond preserving it;
- calculate discounts, taxes, payable cost, or currency conversions;
- implement listing reassignment;
- implement price history, database persistence, retries, or recovery;
- define cart optimization or cross-platform ranking.

## 15. Migration and Database Implications

When the remaining semantic decisions are approved, a durable database may
represent separate domains for canonical identity, listing identity,
observations, typed commercial facts, offers, availability, context, evidence
references, and execution state.

Raw artifacts should remain blob/object storage. Candidate snapshots, matching
results, cost evaluations, and cart plans remain derived unless a later
contract explicitly makes a result durable.

The current filesystem stores remain valid for the local MVP. A database should
not be introduced until the OPEN identity, price, offer, availability, context,
and historical semantics above are resolved.
